# Draft sleepers, 2026/27

Written for **FPL Draft**, not Classic. That distinction drives everything
below, so it's worth stating plainly before the names start.

In Classic, a sleeper is someone cheap who scores. Price is the whole pitch.
In Draft there is no budget: every player costs exactly one pick, so cheapness
is worth precisely nothing. A Draft sleeper is someone who will **out-produce
the pick you have to spend on him**, which comes down to three things:

1. **Nailed minutes.** A rotation risk is unrosterable. You cannot sell him.
2. **A points source that survives his team being bad.** Clean sheets vanish on
   a mid-table defence. Defensive contribution does not.
3. **The market underrating him**, which you read off the Market column on your
   board rather than from any article, including this one.

Season opens **Friday 21 August**, Arsenal v Coventry.

---

## 1. The single biggest edge: defensive contribution

Unchanged for 2026/27. A **defender** banks 2 points for 10+ clearances,
blocks, interceptions and tackles in a match. A **midfielder or forward** needs
12 of those plus ball recoveries. Capped at 2 per match. BPS was retuned this
season specifically to overlap less with these points, which if anything makes
them additive rather than double-counted.

Why this matters more in Draft than Classic: defender scoring is compressed, so
the gap between the best defender and a replacement-level one is small. Anything
that reliably adds ~40 points a season to a defender is enormous in VORP terms.
And it is the one points source that goes **up** when a team is bad.

The metric that matters is **hit rate**, not total actions. A player averaging 9
actions a week scores nothing. One alternating 6 and 12 scores in half his
matches. Same season total, wildly different fantasy output.

**Defenders by hit rate, 2025/26:**

| Player | Club | Hit rate |
|---|---|---|
| Marcos Senesi | Spurs | 70.3% |
| James Hill | Bournemouth | 63.6% |
| Joachim Andersen | Crystal Palace | 60.6% |
| James Tarkowski | Everton | 59.5% |
| Alfie Ballard | Sunderland | 58.3% |

**Midfielders by hit rate, 2025/26:**

| Player | Club | Hit rate |
|---|---|---|
| Elliot Anderson | Man City | 70.3% |
| Danilo Ampadu | Leeds | 54.3% |
| James Garner | Nott'm Forest | 52.6% |
| Kalvin Phillips | Man City | 47.8% |

A 60% hit rate is roughly **45 points a season** before a single clean sheet or
attacking return. Senesi at 70% is closer to 53.

**James Garner is the one to circle.** He hits 52.6% *and* takes Everton's free
kicks and corners, which is a genuinely rare combination: a floor-raiser with a
ceiling attached. Elliot Anderson's 70.3% is the highest of any midfielder, and
his move to Man City should mean more possession and more chances to press.

**Opponents to target** when streaming for defensive points: Crystal Palace (36%
threshold hit rate against, venue-neutral), Bournemouth (39% at home), Everton.
For midfielders, Tottenham at home (25% vs 18% away) and Forest at home (22% vs
11% away).

## 3. League-wide sleepers, by position

The promoted-club stuff is where everyone looks. These are less picked over.

### Goalkeepers — take one late, and make it one of these

Goalkeeper is where the gap between best and replacement is smallest, so never
spend early. But there is still a right way to spend your late pick.

- **Bart Verbruggen** (Brighton) — 130 points last season, best points-per-match
  efficiency of any keeper in his bracket, and strong late-season form.
- **Caoimhin Kelleher** (Brentford) — 143 points, comparable efficiency, on a
  side that concedes enough shots to generate save points.
- **David Raya** (Arsenal) — 162 points and the Golden Glove. He is the actual
  best keeper; the only question is whether you want to spend the pick.

Four keepers changed managers over the summer (Petrovic, Donnarumma, Leno,
Henderson). Past points are a weak guide there.

### Defenders — the floor-raisers

Everything in the defensive-contribution table above. Beyond that:

- **Joachim Andersen** (Crystal Palace, 60.6%) is the sweet spot: high hit rate
  on a side that defends deep enough to sustain it, without being so bad that
  clean sheets never happen.
- **James Tarkowski** (Everton, 59.5%) same profile, and Everton were solid
  defensively.
- **Luka Vuskovic** (Brighton, new) — 12.5 defensive actions per 90 in the
  Bundesliga, projected at 9.9 in the Premier League, which is right on the
  10-action threshold. Also 6 goals and a 74.5% aerial win rate. Rotation risk
  with Struijk and Dunk is the catch. A pure upside pick.

### Midfielders — set pieces plus defensive work

- **James Garner** (Everton) — covered above. Free kicks, corners, 52.6% hit
  rate. The best combination of floor and ceiling outside the elite tier.
- **Mamadou Sangaré** (Brentford, new) — 14.2 defensive actions per 90 in Ligue
  1, projected 12.5 in the Premier League, which would rank **sixth in the
  league**. The 12-action midfielder threshold is hard to clear; he clears it.
  Competition from Janelt, Yarmolyuk and Damsgaard is the risk, so watch the
  first two gameweeks and claim on waivers.
- **Elliot Anderson** (Man City) — highest midfield hit rate in the league at
  70.3%, now on a team that will dominate possession.
- **Danilo Ampadu** (Leeds, 54.3%) — cheap source of the same thing.

### Forwards — penalties or nothing, this late

By the back end of a draft, the forwards left are bad. The only thing that
reliably separates them is spot kicks. From the confirmed lists, late-round
forwards who actually take penalties: **Wood** (Forest), **Solanke**
(Tottenham), **Calvert-Lewin** (Leeds), **Beto** (Everton, third in line),
**McBurnie** (Hull), **Haji Wright** (Coventry), **Kroupi** (Bournemouth,
second).

**Gonzalo García** (Fulham, new) is the interesting one: 6 goals in 9 La Liga
starts, 0.57 goals per 90 for Real Madrid, projected 0.44 in the Premier
League, and he fills the void left by Wilson and Jiménez. Fulham's penalties
are listed as **unsettled**, and he is the most likely resolution. If that
lands he is a steal at wherever he goes.

## 4. Traps

- **Rudoni** (Coventry) — in the predicted XI but was flagged doubtful. Confirm.
- **Dasilva** (Coventry) — starting spot under threat. A benched defender in
  Draft is dead weight you cannot sell.
- **Issa Diop** (Ipswich) — the Classic guides like him, but Kipré and Greaves
  are ahead of him in the predicted XI.
- **Christos Tzolis** (Arsenal, new) — 17 goals and 23 assists at Club Brugge
  looks superb until you see the projection drop to 0.28 goals per 90 from 0.50,
  with Martinelli blocking the minutes. Name value exceeds likely output.
- **Bazoumana Touré** (Newcastle, new) — must displace Harvey Barnes first, and
  his assist projection more than halves. Conservative minutes expected.
- **Any Hull or Coventry asset you need starting in GW1-5.** Both fixture runs
  are brutal. Ipswich is the one promoted side with a kind opening.

## 5. What to actually do with this

**Rounds 1-9:** ignore everything on this page. Take the best available
midfielders and forwards off your board. These names are not competing for
those picks.

**Rounds 10-13:** this is where the defensive-contribution defenders belong.
Andersen, Tarkowski, Senesi, Hill. Garner if he's somehow still there. Your
first keeper.

**Rounds 14-15:** Leif Davis if he lasted (he probably won't), van Ewijk,
McBurnie, Haji Wright, second keeper, Vuskovic as a pure flier.

**The one rule:** never take a promoted-club player before round 11. Their whole
value is that they are free. **Leif Davis is the single exception**, and only by
a round or two.

**Your first three waiver claims, in order:**

1. **Charlie Hughes** (Hull) the moment he's fit. Best defensive-contribution
   profile of any promoted defender, guaranteed to go undrafted because he
   misses GW1.
2. **Mamadou Sangaré** (Brentford) if he starts the first two gameweeks.
   Top-six defensive-contribution rate in the whole league.
3. **Gonzalo García** (Fulham) if he takes a penalty in the opening weeks.
